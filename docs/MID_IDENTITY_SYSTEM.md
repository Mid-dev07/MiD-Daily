# MiD Daily — Core Identity System

Status: Identity Lock v1.0

This document defines the product identity that must remain stable across future UI/UX redesigns, feature growth, integrations, and visual experiments.

UNREAL is an execution and spatial-quality layer. It is not MiD's identity.

## 01 — Identity in One Sentence

MiD is a living personal operating workspace that helps a person understand the shape of their day, decide what matters, and act without friction.

The product is not a generic dashboard, planner, finance tracker, AI assistant, or game-like interface.

Those are capabilities inside the same workspace.

## 02 — The MiD Idea

MiD should feel like:

> a personal room for the day, engineered with the precision of a good instrument.

The room is calm. The instrument is precise. The environment is alive. The information is useful. The interface stays quiet until something deserves attention.

This creates the central tension that makes MiD distinctive:

Human atmosphere × engineered clarity.

## 03 — Core Identity Pillars

### 1. Living
MiD reflects the state of the person's day instead of presenting the same static dashboard every time.

Signals may come from current time, schedule density, task workload, finance state, habits, assistant context, and local environment.

The interface may respond subtly, but it must never perform for the sake of novelty.

### 2. Grounded
MiD should feel calm, credible, physical, and usable.

The visual foundation is dark mineral surfaces, restrained natural atmosphere, directional light, clear typography, measurable spacing, and purposeful depth.

Avoid visual language that makes MiD feel cyberpunk, arcade-like, futuristic for its own sake, luxury-fashion decorative, or sterile enterprise SaaS.

### 3. Personal
The workspace belongs to the user.

Personalization should appear through real information, workload, schedule, habits, environment, preferences, and identity.

Do not manufacture personality with random gradients or decorative noise.

### 4. Clear
MiD reduces the cognitive work required to understand the day.

The preferred sequence is: recognize → understand → act.

Never force: search → decode → compare → guess.

### 5. Quietly Premium
MiD should feel expensive because every decision is deliberate, not because the interface is visually loud.

Premium comes from spacing, hierarchy, typography, material behavior, consistency, transitions, and restraint.

## 04 — Brand Personality

MiD is:

Calm — never frantic, never noisy, never demanding.
Intelligent — notices patterns, surfaces relevant context, does not pretend to know everything.
Grounded — practical, human, physically believable.
Precise — measured, structured, consistent.
Alive — responds to the state of the day; changes through meaningful context.
Confident — does not need excessive decoration to prove quality.

### Personality formula
70% calm + 20% precision + 10% surprise.

The surprise must be contextual, not random.

## 05 — Emotional Target

A successful MiD session should leave the user feeling:
- I understand my day.
- I know what deserves attention.
- I can act immediately.
- This feels like my space.
- The interface feels good without getting in my way.

MiD should not make the user feel overwhelmed, watched, gamified, buried in dashboards, forced to configure everything, or impressed by effects before understanding the product.

## 06 — Identity Hierarchy

The following order is mandatory:

1. Product — daily management and personal agency.
2. Experience — a calm, living workspace.
3. Visual language — dark mineral materials + natural atmosphere + measured light.
4. Interaction language — local response, depth, clarity, useful motion.
5. UNREAL layer — high-fidelity material and spatial execution.

This order must never be inverted.

## 07 — Visual DNA

Base: MiD dark mineral base #0a1119.

Surface family: deep blue-grey / mineral materials.

Signature accent: electric cyan #4fd1ff. Cyan is a signature interaction language, not a page color.

Semantic states: success #4ade80, warning #fbbf24, danger #fb7185.

Key light: 145deg. The same directional logic governs highlights, raised edges, bevel-like gradients, local reflections, and selected surfaces.

Natural atmosphere remains part of MiD's identity, but it belongs primarily to the environmental/contextual layer, not the whole UI palette.

Use moss, sage, stone, fog, and muted warm sunlight to support mood and depth rather than recolor the product.

## 08 — Material Identity

MiD has four material families:

Mineral — structural, neutral, grounded. Used for navigation shell, structural surfaces, quiet containers.

Glass — translucent, contextual, atmospheric. Used selectively for panels and transient surfaces.

Recessed — inward, focused, quiet. Used for inputs, search, data wells, and secondary controls.

Emissive — focused, meaningful, limited. Used for primary actions, active state, progress, live status, and important attention signals.

No surface should receive a material merely because the material exists.

## 09 — Composition Language

MiD should not be a wall of cards.

Preferred composition: one strong focal region, clear working region, small supporting signals, quiet ambient environment.

Use asymmetry, negative space, controlled alignment, editorial hierarchy, spatial navigation, and progressive disclosure.

Avoid identical card grids, equal visual weight everywhere, giant hero sections on every view, repeated icon/title/text blocks, and decorative empty space without purpose.

## 11 — Feature Landscape

Feature Landscape is a brand-level experience element.

It represents the idea that every part of the person's life has a place inside one connected workspace.

In the spatial direction, this principle extends from a single component into the environment itself: the workspace should feel like one connected place, while navigation and information remain DOM-first.

It must feel spatial, calm, discoverable, slightly surprising, and useful.

It must not become a game map, visual gimmick, or main navigation replacement.

## 12 — Environment Identity

The living environment is MiD's ambient world.

It communicates time, mood, natural context, and progression through the day.

It should stay behind the information layer.

Foreground rule: content wins over atmosphere.

Environment changes must be state-driven, slow, subdued, lightweight, and reduced-motion safe.

## 13 — Module Personalities

All modules share MiD identity.

Today — orientation — cyan.
Schedule — rhythm — violet.
Tasks — progress — green.
Finance — resources — amber.
Social — signal — rose.
Assistant — guidance — cyan-light.
Profile — identity — silver.
Insights — observation — indigo.
Habits — cultivation — growth green.

Local personality may change indicator, active edge, progress, small light response, and local emphasis.

It must never produce nine unrelated themes.

## 14 — Typography Identity

MiD typography should feel editorial and engineered.

Primary UI: Inter or equivalent neutral sans.
Display: strong hierarchy, restrained usage.
Mono: indices, timestamps, telemetry, and technical metadata.

Typography should communicate hierarchy before decoration.

## 15 — Interaction Identity

Hover — local light catch and small elevation change.
Focus — visible, high-contrast focus boundary.
Active — small physical press.
Selected — local emissive rim and stronger contrast.
Disabled — reduced energy, never ambiguity.

Motion exists to explain state or causality.

Preferred ranges: micro 120–160ms, normal 180–240ms, scene 260–360ms.

## 16 — Language / Copy Identity

MiD copy is short, direct, human, calm.

Prefer: Today, Next up, Open tasks, Nothing overdue, Your schedule is clear, Focus on this next.

Avoid inflated marketing language, unnecessary technical jargon, artificial motivational language, excessive uppercase, and robotic status messages.

The product should sound like a capable quiet assistant, not a sales page.

## 17 — Logo / Mark Direction

The current M monogram is retained as a placeholder, not considered final brand identity.

The final mark should communicate three concepts:

M + movement + personal space.

Desired properties:
- recognizable at 16–32px;
- geometric enough for an app icon;
- organic enough to avoid generic SaaS appearance;
- simple enough to work as SVG;
- strong in monochrome;
- compatible with the dark mineral foundation.

Do not finalize the logo through decorative gradients alone.

The brand mark must work without color.

## 18 — What MiD Is Not

MiD is not a cyberpunk command console, GTA-like interface, generic productivity SaaS, finance dashboard with other tabs, AI wrapper, nature wallpaper app, glassmorphism showcase, gamified habit tracker, or futuristic UI portfolio piece.

UNREAL references may influence material quality, but they do not define MiD's character.

## 19 — Identity Test

Before a future feature or redesign ships, ask:

Product test — Does this make the user's day easier to understand or manage?
Identity test — Would this still feel like MiD if the logo were removed?
Material test — Does the visual treatment support hierarchy?
Context test — Does atmosphere respond to actual state?
Restraint test — Would removing the effect make the product worse?
Recognition test — Could a user recognize MiD from a partial screenshot?

If the recognition test fails, the work requires revision.

## 20 — Canonical MiD Definition

MiD is a living personal operating workspace: a calm, precise space where schedule, tasks, money, habits, insights, and connected tools come together around the reality of one person's day.

Its signature is not a color, a glass effect, or a futuristic animation.

Its signature is:

calm structure + living context + engineered material quality + personal relevance.

UNREAL exists to make that signature tangible.
